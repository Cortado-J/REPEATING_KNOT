# Repeating Knot Cards

**[Download Knot-Cards.pdf](Knot-Cards.pdf)**: Repeating Knot Cards, a pack of 60 Celtic knot cards to print and cut out.
**[Download Knot-Cards-solutions.pdf](Knot-Cards-solutions.pdf)**: the answers, kept apart.
Their home, with a card table to try layouts on screen and a guide to the groups, is
[knotworthy.co.uk/K2026-06](https://knotworthy.co.uk/K2026-06).

Every card is a little patch of knotwork, and every one can be turned any way: wherever it goes, the ribbons
still pass alternately over and under. Lay four cards in a square, repeat the square, and the knot takes on one
of nine kinds of symmetry: nine of the seventeen wallpaper groups.

There are three challenges. Easy: read a target card's back (its group, with its mirrors, glides and turn
centres marked), then build it and find each mark. Medium: build a target from the four letters on its front and
work out its group. Hard: find all nine groups with the pattern cards alone.

## What is in the PDFs

The cards (Knot-Cards.pdf, 7 pages):

1. The instructions: a leaflet of 20 panels (the four kinds of card, how to play, the three challenges, how to read
   the names) that folds along its dotted lines to lie on the cards in the box.
2. Pages 2–5: four sheets of 12 pattern cards (48 in all: 16 T, 16 G, 8 R, 8 E).
3. Page 6: nine target cards and three reference cards (rules, how to read the backs, questions to wonder about).
4. Page 7: the box, a lid and a tray.

The solutions (Knot-Cards-solutions.pdf, 2 pages): the nine targets at a glance, each with its group and marks,
and the backs of the target and reference cards.

## Printing

- A4, at 100% ("actual size", not "fit to page"). Page 1, the instructions, on ordinary paper: zig-zag it along the
  dotted lines, across first, then down. The other pages on heavy card, 300 gsm or more. Cards are 60 mm square;
  cut on the crop marks.
- For double-sided targets, print page 2 of the solutions on the back of page 6 of the cards, flipping on the
  long edge.
- The box: glue its page onto thin card, such as a cereal box. Cut on the solid lines, score and fold the dashed
  ones, and glue each flap inside the wall it meets. The lid fits over the tray, and the box holds the cards with the folded instructions on top.
